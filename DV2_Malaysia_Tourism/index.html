Malaysia Tourism Recovery and Regional Differences

Malaysia's tourism industry experienced a severe decline during the travel disruption period, especially around 2020 and 2021. This visualisation focuses on the recovery stage: how international visitors returned, how domestic tourism rebounded, and how recovery patterns differed across Malaysian states.

Recovery Was Uneven Across Malaysian States

The map below shows the average occupancy rate of hotels by state in 2024. Higher occupancy rates suggest stronger accommodation demand, but the spatial pattern shows that tourism recovery was not equally distributed across all states.

The ranking chart makes the state differences easier to compare. It shows which states recorded higher hotel occupancy rates and which states remained lower in 2024.

The chart below compares domestic and foreign hotel guests by state in 2024. It shows that some states relied more on domestic guests, while Kuala Lumpur had a much larger share of foreign hotel guests.

Domestic Tourism Also Recovered Strongly

The two charts below show that domestic tourism expenditure and domestic visitor numbers followed almost the same recovery pattern. Both dropped sharply in 2020 and 2021, then rebounded strongly from 2022 onwards, reaching their highest levels in 2024.

The bar chart below highlights how sharply domestic tourism changed from year to year, including the severe decline in 2020–2021 and the strong rebound in 2022.

Key Takeaways

Malaysia's tourism recovery was strong, but not uniform. International arrivals returned from key source markets, domestic tourism expenditure and visitor numbers recovered together, and hotel performance varied clearly across states. This suggests that Malaysia's tourism recovery should be understood not only as a national rebound, but also as a pattern shaped by source markets, seasons and regional differences.
